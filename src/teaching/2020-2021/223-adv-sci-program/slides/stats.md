## Statistics
* The practice or science of collecting and analyzing numerical data in large quantities.
* *Data* comes from observations, counts, measurements, or responses.
* The *population* is the collection of all data.
* A *sample* is a subset of the population.
* *Parameters* are a numerical description of a population characteristic. 
* A *statistic* is a numerical description of a simple characteristic.

---

## Branches of Statistics
* *Descriptive Statistics* involves the organization, summarization, and display of data.
* *Inferential Statistics* involves using a sample to draw conclusions about a population. One of the basic tools used is probability.

---

## Data Classification
* Data can be classified into two types:
    * *Qualitative Data* consists of attributes, labels, or nonnumerical entries. 
    * *Quantitative Data* consists of numerical measurements or counts.
* Data Types:
    * *Categorical*
        * *Nominal*: Categorized using names, labels, or qualities.
        * *Ordinal*: Data where the order is important (Ranked or Sorted Data)
    * *Numerical*
        * *Discrete*: Data that can't be measured, but counted.
        * *Continuous*: Can't be counted, but measured
---

## Measures of Central Tendency
* *Mean* - Sum of dataset divided by the number of entries.
* *Median* - Value that lies in the middle when data is in sorted order.
* *Mode* - Data entry that occurs the most.

---

* *Outlier* - Entry far removed from the other entries in the dataset.
* *Weighted Mean* - Mean of a dataset whose entries have varying weights.

---

## Measures of Variation

* The *range* of a dataset is the difference between the max and min data entries.
* *Deviation* of an entry in a population dataset is the difference between the entry and the mean (μ *mu*).
