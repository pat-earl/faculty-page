## Statistics
* The practice or science of collecting and analyzing numerical data in large quantities.
* *Data* comes from observations, counts, measurements, or responses.
* The *population* is the collection of all data.
* A *sample* is a subset of the population.
* *Parameters* are a numerical description of a population characteristic. 
* A *statistic* is a numerical description of a simple characteristic.

---

## Two Branches

* *Descriptive Statistics*
* *Inferential Statistics*

---

## Descriptive Statistics

* Used to summarize and describe data. 
    * A number that describes another grouping of numbers
* Examples:
    * The percentage of a state's education budget that goes to one specific district.
    * Average age of students per classification. 
* They **don't** generalize the data beyond what's at hand. 

---

## Inferential Statistics
* Typically used to make generalizations about a population from a sample
* Since samples are a small subset of a population, samples have to be selected carefully.
* We could call this forecasting and won't make it our main focus for visualizations. 

---

## Variables

* Properties or characteristics of some event, object, or person that can take on different values (or amounts).
* Variables can be two types:
    * *Independent* - Manipulated by the experimenter (What happens if we change this?)
    * *Dependent* - Measures the experimental outcome (Did changing the independent variable change anything?)

---

## Data Classification
* Data (Variables) can be classified into two types:
    * *Qualitative Data* consists of attributes, labels, or nonnumerical entries. (Order isn't implied)
    * *Quantitative Data* consists of numerical measurements or counts.
* Data Types:
    * *Categorical*
        * *Nominal*: Categorized using names, labels, or qualities.
        * *Ordinal*: Data where the order is important (Ranked or Sorted Data)
    * *Numerical*
        * *Discrete*: Data that can't be measured, but counted.
        * *Continuous*: Can't be counted, but measured
  
---

## Distributions

* *Discrete Variables*
    * *Frequency Tables* can show the number of times a discrete value appears
* *Continuous Variables*
    * *Grouped Frequency Distributions* - Counting the frequency a value appears in a pre-determined ranged.
    * Can be shown using a *histogram*.

---

## Measures of Central Tendency

* *Mean* - Sum of dataset divided by the number of entries.
* *Median* - Value that lies in the middle when data is in sorted order.
* *Mode* - Data entry that occurs the most.

---

* *Outlier* - Entry far removed from the other entries in the dataset.
* *Weighted Mean* - Mean of a dataset whose entries have varying weights.

---

## Measures of Variation

* How much spread is there in a group of data?
* The *range* of a dataset is the difference between the max and min data entries.
* *Variance* of an entry is how close a score is to the middle of a distribution. 
* *Deviation* of an entry in a population dataset is the difference between the entry and the mean.
